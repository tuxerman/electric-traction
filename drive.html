Drive it: 0 to 110 km/h

Each loco pulls the same train: 22 LHB coaches, about 1050 t, on level track. Each loco has its own driver switch. On Auto the computer drives: it notches the WAP4 the way crews are taught, keeping motor current under 900 A and motor voltage under 750 V and using field weakening after notch 20. It asks the WAP7 for full tractive effort. Switch either loco to Me and drive it yourself, for example against the computer on the other loco, then press Start from 0. Raise or lower the pantograph voltage at any time and watch what each loco does with it. This is an illustrative model built from published ratings, not a certified simulation.

      Inside the loco

      Why the WAP4 line is a sawtooth. Each notch fixes the DC voltage. As the train speeds up, the series motors generate more back-voltage, so less current flows; and because their field is made by that same current, the pull falls even faster than the current does. Taking the next notch lifts the voltage and the current jumps back up. The WAP7's converters re-adjust voltage and frequency hundreds of times a second to hold the pull you asked for, so its line stays flat until it reaches its power limit.
